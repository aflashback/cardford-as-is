# Spades strategy research and evaluation

This note combines a read-only review of the current strategy code and CR-STRAT-009 with primary research. It does not report new tests, live Jev results or demonstrated playing strength. The two-deal reasoning-agent pilot is an **authored scenario experiment**. It can expose decisions worth investigating; it cannot estimate win rates, probabilities, natural error frequency or superiority over the production bot.

The runtime Jev boundary is unchanged. These reasoning agents are development participants, not shipped players or coaches. This experiment adds no runtime model and makes no live Jev request. The selectable code policies remain subject to the existing promotion requirements.

## What is already useful

The implementation has a frozen D0 comparison policy, common sampled worlds for candidate comparisons, deterministic work budgets, seat-scoped continuation behavior, separate Solo and Partners objectives, explicit uncertainty/abstention records, and seed-clustered gate inference. These are useful foundations. Legality, information isolation and scoring correctness remain separate from strategic quality.

## Five gaps to close

1. **Complete the planned evidence pipeline.** At review time, the checkout contained gate machinery but no CR-STRAT-009 case store, committed gate manifests or completed gate reports; the gate ledger's consumed list was empty. Preserve the distinction between implemented machinery and executed acceptance evidence. Finish replayable cases, blind review packets and sealed confirmation runs before considering promotion. [Change record](C:/repos/test-jev/planning/decisions/CR-STRAT-009-game-plan-and-strength-gate.md), [ledger](C:/repos/test-jev/qa/evaluation/gate/ledger-state.json).

2. **Broaden evaluation beyond one policy family.** The six named styles reweight D0's effects and modify its bidding. They offer behavioral variation, but share substantial assumptions and blind spots. Include at least one independently authored code policy and report performance by opponent population. More self-play or more variants of the same weights do not by themselves establish robustness. [Styles](C:/repos/test-jev/web/server/games/spades/evaluation/styles.ts).

3. **Give unfamiliar partners a promotion guard.** The code already includes one-seat, both-teammates and unfamiliar-teammate placements. However, the primary Partners estimand uses both teammates running the evaluated policy; other configurations are descriptive. Add a prespecified unfamiliar-partner guard and examine harmful interactions separately. Self-play success can depend on conventions that fail with new partners: this is demonstrated in Hanabi research, and is a reason to test Spades cross-play rather than an established Spades result. [Placements](C:/repos/test-jev/web/server/games/spades/evaluation/placements.ts), [Other-Play](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/hu20a.html).

4. **Separate positive bidding from Nil inference.** The current world weight applies a Gaussian distance from one uncalibrated trick estimate to every observed bid, including zero. Its continuation bidder also uses a simple Nil threshold. Compare uniform and bid-weighted worlds, test Nil-specific likelihood assumptions, and evaluate bidding changes while holding card play fixed. The partnership-only *Bidding in Spades* study uses controlled bidder comparisons and explicitly models Nil and endgame context; its reported performance cannot be transferred to CardFord or Solo. [Model](C:/repos/test-jev/web/server/games/spades/playout/model.ts), [bidder](C:/repos/test-jev/web/server/games/spades/playout/policy.ts), [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.11323v2).

5. **Expand genuine plan tradeoffs.** Alternative stances currently cover avoiding bags versus taking/denying tricks. Other decisions continue under a fixed heuristic policy. Build reachable cases for protecting Nil versus making the positive contract, sacrificing one of two Nils, attacking an opposing Nil versus its coverer, and preserving entries. Compare alternatives over the same sampled worlds. A hand-length rollout does not establish that these plans are well represented. [Planning pack](C:/repos/test-jev/web/server/games/spades/plan/pack.ts).

## Additional findings

**Search quality depends on its assumptions.** More sampled worlds reduce sampling noise, not errors in the allocation model or continuation policy. Compare multiple continuation assumptions and retain hidden-hand permutation checks. AI Factory's own bidding-inference report warns that models learned from self-play principally reflect that program's bidding. Its proprietary implementation is a research reference, not code to copy. [AI Factory report](https://www.aifactory.co.uk/newsletter/2019_01_bidding_inferences.htm).

**A tree-search rewrite needs evidence.** ISMCTS addresses some strategy-fusion problems by sharing information-set search statistics, but its advantage varies by game and branching structure. Keep actor-safe flat playouts while collecting failures that justify deeper search. Neither sampled perfect-information solutions nor access to the actual hidden deal are valid substitutes for player knowledge. [ISMCTS paper](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/75048/1/CowlingPowleyWhitehouse2012.pdf).

**One coach sentence overstates its comparison.** The sampled reason says the selected choice “did no worse” than alternatives, although tactical tie-breaking may select a lower sampled mean within the comparable band. Wording such as “The samples did not clearly separate these choices” better matches that situation. Verify the rendered cue path before calling this an observed UI defect. [Reason text](C:/repos/test-jev/web/server/games/spades/plan/profiles.ts).

**Bot strength and coach quality require different evidence.** AI Factory's commercial Spades study distinguishes objective playing strength from conspicuous decisions that damage perceived partner quality. CardFord should measure factual accuracy, action/explanation agreement and abstention alongside outcomes, then test whether people learn. [Commercial Spades study](https://edpowley.com/academic/papers/aiide13.pdf).

## Practical measurement ladder

1. **Authored cases: discovery and regression.** Give each player only its own hand, public history, rules and legal actions. Keep the observer non-intervening. Replay actions through the production engine. Record short decision summaries, rejected actions, contradictions and plan revisions. Judge decisions using information available then; label later outcome analysis as hindsight. Convert useful failures into cases with Solo/Partners twins where applicable. Agent agreement is authored evidence, not human expert validation. A successful replay establishes that this trajectory is legal, not that its decisions are strong.

2. **Fresh deals: comparative strength.** Freeze policies, rules, objectives, budgets, manifests and decision criteria before confirmation. Use paired deals, seat rotations, fresh unread seed ranges and clustered intervals. Include independent opponents, unfamiliar partners, in-match states and complete-match guards. Report Solo and Partners separately: score margin, contract/Nil outcomes, bags, changed decisions, subgroup harm, factual errors and abstention. Keep discovered or tuned-on deals out of confirmation. A hand-margin improvement supports a scoped scoring claim; match strength needs match evidence.

3. **Humans: coaching transfer.** Test experienced players on fresh decisions before coaching, during assistance and later without assistance. Track assistance exposure; compare delayed unaided performance and factual understanding against a suitable control. Helpful-sounding explanations, stronger bots, agent consensus and assisted success do not demonstrate learning. Human review also remains distinct from the owner approval needed to promote a live default under CR-STRAT-009.
